# Binary Amplitude Diffuser

A surface of small raised elements (often wood cubes or squares) in a grid pattern, where each element is at one of two heights, creating a two-level amplitude/phase pattern.

- Pattern derived from Thue-Morse / binary sequences for a flat reflection spectrum.
- Material: Wood, MDF, or composite blocks.
- Typical depth: 5–15 cm between the two levels.
- Effect: Diffuses and attenuates high-frequency reflections; the two-level surface scatters mid- and high-frequencies.

Often used on rear walls and ceiling surfaces where a less aggressive look is wanted. Shallow versions mainly act as a comb of high-frequency scatterers rather than true broadband diffusers.
